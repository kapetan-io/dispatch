1. **Orient.** Read the ticket, its comments, and the design it cites. Don't re-argue the design. The ticket's statements of fact are guesses until you observe them. Its cause, repro, description of today's code, test recipe and what it says the design requires are claims. Check each claim your work depends on by running it, writing a failing test, or tracing the code. Settle a design claim by finding the sentence in the design that says it. A section on the topic that never states the rule does not support it. A claim that checks out needs no comment. For a bug, write the failing surface test and see it fail before you edit any source. If it doesn't fail on main, write no fix, post what you ran and saw, and move the ticket to Needs Human. When a claim is wrong, ask: once corrected, can you still deliver what the ticket asks for, with its acceptance criteria met as written? A wrong file, helper, command or recipe leaves the ask intact. Comment your evidence on the ticket, create what is missing or work around it, and keep going. Stop only when the correction means the ticket asks for something else. A limit, floor, range, default or allowed value the design never states is that case: picking it is a product call, not yours. Quote the ticket's claim next to what you found in a ticket comment, with what you ran and saw rather than guesses, and move the ticket to Needs Human. Write the comment before you build on the claim. Every acceptance criterion must be observable at the surface. If one isn't, ask. A ticket already In Review has its PR open, so skip to step 7.
