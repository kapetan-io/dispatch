1. **Orient.** Read the ticket, its comments, and the design it cites. The design decides what to build, so don't re-argue it. The ticket's statements of fact are guesses until you observe them. It was often written by an agent from memory, so its cause, repro, description of today's code, test recipe and what it says the design requires are claims. Check each claim your work depends on by running it, writing a failing test, or tracing the code. Check a design claim against the design itself. Skip claims that don't carry weight. A claim that checks out needs no comment. For a bug, write the failing surface test first and run it to see it fail before you edit any source. If it doesn't fail on main, write no fix, post what you ran on the ticket, and move it to Needs Human. When a claim is wrong, ask one question: once corrected, can you still deliver what the ticket asks for, with its acceptance criteria met as written? A wrong file, helper, command or recipe leaves the ask intact. Comment on the ticket with your evidence, create what is missing or work around it, and keep going. Only stop when the correction means the ticket asks for something else, such as a requirement the design never makes. Then quote the ticket's claim next to what you saw in a ticket comment and move the ticket to Needs Human. Your final message is not the ticket, so write the comment before you build on the claim. Every acceptance criterion must be observable at the surface. If one isn't, ask. A ticket already In Review has its PR open, so skip to step 7.
